ShopAssist AI — RAG + multi-agent support assistant

Customer-support assistant with retrieval, tool calling and an agentic dispute investigator that is checked by a separate verifier agent.

  • ONNX intent router: 99.8% accuracy, ~1 ms, 429 KB
  • Multi-agent investigation: 7/7 cases vs 3/7 single-agent

FastAPIChromaONNXClaude / OpenAI-compatibleDocker

Problem

Support bots either hallucinate or cannot act. The goal was an assistant that grounds answers in a knowledge base, calls real tools (order lookup, ticket creation) and can investigate a dispute over multiple steps without going off the rails.

What I built

  • FastAPI service with a Streamlit UI; Chroma vector store using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 on onnxruntime for retrieval.
  • Tiny ONNX intent router (429 KB, ~1 ms) in front of the LLM: 0.998 accuracy, 0.9985 macro-F1.
  • Provider-agnostic LLM layer (Anthropic Claude or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint such as Groq, Gemini, OpenRouter, vLLM, Ollama) with a fallback ladder, tenacity retries, token-bucket rate limiting and a TTL cache exposed via an X-Cache header.

  • Agentic dispute investigation (POST /investigate): up to 10 steps with 3 investigative and 3 terminal tools, a separate verifier sub-agent, per-role token accounting, context clearing, repeat detection and fault injection.

Research · Prixa Student Collaborator · 2025–26

Muzzle-based biometric identification for cattle in Nepal

Ear tags fall off or get swapped, so farmers, insurers and vets have no reliable way to identify an individual animal. A muzzle print is unique like a fingerprint. I collected a farm dataset, compared classical feature matching with deep metric learning, and shipped a mobile app to register and identify animals by camera.

86.6%Top-1 across 455 identities
0.037equal-error rate · AUC 0.988
90cattle photographed on farms
TensorFlow / KerasResNet-50 + ArcFaceAlbumentationsLBP · LPQ · HOG · GaborOpenCVFlutter

Problem

Smallholder farmers and insurers in Nepal need a cheap, tamper-proof way to identify individual cattle. Ear tags fall off and get swapped; muzzle patterns do not change.

Data

  • Collected a custom dataset by visiting farms across the Kathmandu valley: 90 cattle, each with full-face, left/right body and multiple muzzle images.
  • Trained and evaluated on two muzzle datasets: 455 identities / 2,747 images (long-tailed, 55 single-image classes) and 268 identities / 4,923 images.
  • Per-class split rules for tiny classes and offline Albumentations augmentation (random resized crops, flips, shift/scale/rotate, brightness/contrast, noise, blur, gamma, coarse dropout) to guarantee at least 10 training images per identity.

Approaches

  • Classical: LBP, LPQ, HOG, Gabor filters and blob detection feeding heuristic matching and an SVM.
  • Deep metric learning: ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 backbone with a 512-d embedding and an ArcFace head (s=30, m=0.35), PK-sampled batches, two-stage training (frozen backbone, then last 50 layers unfrozen). Compared Adam, AdamW with balanced class weights and RectifiedAdam.
  • Also benchmarked ResNet-18, MobileNet, DenseNet and a Swin Transformer as backbones.

Results (455-identity test set)

  • Top-1 86.55%, Top-5 94.18%, balanced accuracy 84.5%, macro-F1 0.81.
  • Verification: EER 0.037, ROC-AUC 0.988.

Product

  • Flutter mobile app to register new cattle and identify existing ones through the camera.

Research · Medical imaging · CNN + U-Net

Skin lesion classification & segmentation

Classifies dermoscopy images into 7 lesion types and segments the lesion region, served through a Flask app that returns class, calibrated confidence, malignancy note and a mask.

  • HAM10000: 10,015 images, 7 classes, class-balanced to 5,000/class
  • U-Net with Dice + focal-Tversky losses on 10,015 image/mask pairs
  • Custom CNN vs ResNet-50 vs MobileNet transfer-learning comparison
TensorFlow / KerasU-NetFlaskNumPy

Problem

Early triage of skin lesions depends on expert dermatologists who are scarce outside cities. The model gives a first-pass class and highlights the affected region.

What I built

  • Classification: custom CNN over the 7 HAM10000 classes (bkl, nv, df, mel, vasc, bcc, akiec). Heavy class imbalance handled by resampling and augmenting every class to 5,000 images (rotation, shift, shear, flip, contrast) with a 70:20:10 split; compared against ResNet-50 and MobileNet transfer learning.
  • Segmentation: custom U-Net trained on 10,015 image/mask pairs with combined Dice and focal-Tversky loss.
  • Serving: Flask app that loads both models, resizes uploads to 256×256, returns the top class with temperature-scaled softmax confidence, lesion metadata (full name, malignancy, recommendation) and a thresholded binary mask rendered inline. Seeds fixed for determinism.

Hackathon · Protobytes 2.0 · Feb 2026

Universal IMU-to-health pipeline

Self-supervised motion embeddings that standardise accelerometer data across wearables, with fall detection and activity recognition on top of a frozen encoder.

  • 83.0% overall accuracy across three device datasets
  • 96.3% on the Fall class; 95.6% on Walking
  • 1D-CNN encoder + NT-Xent contrastive loss, Streamlit dashboard
PyTorchContrastive learningStreamlitUMAP / t-SNE

Problem

Every wearable samples motion differently, so health models trained on one device fail on another. The team (Coder Rex, 4 ACEM students) built a device-agnostic embedding so one classifier can serve many devices.

What we built

  • IMUEncoder: 1D-CNN (3 → 32 → 64 → 128 channels, global average pooling, projection head) producing 128-d embeddings, trained with NT-Xent contrastive loss (temperature 0.5).
  • MLP fall-detection classifier and a multiclass activity classifier on frozen embeddings.
  • Datasets: SisFall, WISDM, PAMAP2 plus smartwatch/phone samples; Streamlit dashboard with t-SNE/UMAP views of the embedding space.

Results

  • Overall 83.0% accuracy; per device SisFall 82.4%, WISDM 88.6%, PAMAP2 69.4%.
  • Fall 96.3%, Walking 95.6%; Sitting is the hard class at 42.5% (the next thing to fix).

Mobile · Flutter · on-device

Similar image detection app

Flags duplicate or near-duplicate photos before you save them, entirely on-device with no model download and no backend.

  • Perceptual hashing: pHash + dHash compared by Hamming distance
  • Tiered verdicts: exact duplicate (≤3) vs very likely duplicate (≤5)
  • Single- and multi-image picking; hashes persisted locally
Flutter / Dartimageimage_pickershared_preferences

Problem

Phones fill up with repeated shots. Cloud-based duplicate detection costs money and privacy; this app shows the same result can be had with a few lines of signal processing on the device.

How it works

  • Computes an 8×8 grayscale average hash (pHash) and a 9×8 gradient hash (dHash) for each image with the image package.
  • Compares against every previously stored hash by Hamming distance: ≤3 on both hashes is an exact duplicate, ≤5 is a very likely duplicate.
  • Pick one or many photos via image_picker; a dialog warns or confirms, then the hash is persisted with shared_preferences.
